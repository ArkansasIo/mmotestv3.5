# Eldoria: Realms at War — World Bible

This is the shared setting reference for the **Age of Embers**. It expands the names already present in the game into a usable fantasy canon. The catalog is worldbuilding, not a promise that every people, spell, or region is playable in the current client.

## The Premise

Eldoria is a land of old roads, young crowns, and ley-lines that run beneath forests, mountains, rivers, and ruined keeps. Magic is real, but never effortless. A ward needs a boundary; a healing rite needs care; a road-spell needs a road to follow.

The current era is called the **Age of Embers**. It began after the Emberfall, a season of distant fires and failing waystones that severed several ancient routes. The event ended, but its quiet consequences remain: abandoned keeps, wounded ley-lines, unsettled borders, and relics whose makers are forgotten.

No single people caused the Emberfall, and no single crown can repair Eldoria alone. The struggle of the age is whether its rulers will rebuild through treaties and shared knowledge or turn every old wound into a claim for more land.

| People | Homeland | Strength | Tension |

| **Ashen Dominion** | The Cinder March and western highlands | Forgeworks, discipline, and a culture that prizes endurance | A unified war-crown must keep its clans from mistaking strength for entitlement |
| **Ironroot Clans** | The Deepdelve Holds beneath the mountains | Master smiths, stonework, engineering, and carefully kept runes | The clans guard craft knowledge closely, even when neighboring holds need it |
| **Dragonborn Covenant** | The Starfall Crags and high passes | Draconic lore, oathkeeping, and fortress wards | Ancient obligations can bind the living to promises made by ancestors |

These are cultural identities, not moral alignments. Each includes generous neighbors, hard rulers, dissenters, and people who do not fit the stereotype.

## Champions of the Age

These names appear in the current sample rankings, messages, or player profile. The short descriptions below establish story hooks; they do not lock players into a single campaign outcome.

- **Queen Elowen Vale, Sylvan Court:** a careful sovereign who sends gifts and warnings before she sends a warband. She believes a forest boundary is a promise to the next generation.
- **Prince Kael Emberwing, Ashen Dominion:** a young banner-holder whose courage wins followers faster than his patience wins treaties.
- **Warden Aria Vale, Kingdom of Valewyn:** the sample campaign's border warden and a flexible player-facing hero, not a fixed protagonist every player must become.
- **High Marshal Darius Blackthorn, Ashen Dominion:** a disciplined commander who trusts supply lines, prepared defenses, and the word given before a battle.
- **Thane Brokk Ironroot, Ironroot Clans:** a veteran of the Deepdelve Holds who judges a craft by whether it survives a hard winter.
- **Matriarch Vexa Ashhand, Ashen Dominion:** a clan elder who remembers the cost of old feuds and expects young leaders to prove their promises.

The Dragonborn Covenant speaks through an Oath Table rather than a single permanent monarch. Its seat rotates among keepers of the Starfall vows, preventing one bloodline from claiming every ancestral oath.

## The Nine Crowns

The game contains nine political traditions. A crown is a way of organizing power, not a separate species or an automatically virtuous faction.

1. **Free Cities Compact:** chartered towns govern through reeves, guild councils, and negotiated common law.
2. **Iron Crown War Council:** marshal-lords coordinate defense and musters; its challenge is preventing emergency rule from becoming permanent.
3. **Arcane Conclave:** lorekeepers oversee public study, careful experimentation, healing arts, and wardcraft.
4. **The High Kingdom:** one sovereign crown binds noble houses and border keeps through fealty and stewardship.
5. **Amber Road Merchant Houses:** caravan families and market halls turn safe passage and enforceable contracts into influence.
6. **Radiant Synod:** temple leaders and oathbound champions serve the sun-banner, balancing doctrine with mercy.
7. **Mycelium Court:** a woodland compact values shared memory, reciprocal harvest, and care for living ground.
8. **Free March Clans:** independent valleys and hill clans cooperate for mutual defense while guarding local custom.
9. **Noble Houses of Dawn:** hereditary houses use patronage, heraldry, and courtly alliances to compete for influence.

## The Nine Great Realms

The political map is commonly spoken of as nine great realms. Each is a broad region containing smaller holdings and border marches; their borders are debated and can change.

1. **Valewyn Crownlands:** river valleys, orchard towns, old royal roads, and the stone seat at Eastridge.
2. **Silverwood Reach:** moonlit groves, living bridges, and watchtowers grown around elder trees.
3. **Cinder March:** basalt ridges, charcoal forests, furnace towns, and the long road to the Ashen forges.
4. **Deepdelve Holds:** mountain halls connected by marked tunnels, underground rivers, and sealed rune doors.
5. **Starfall Crags:** high plateaus, wind-carved shrines, dragon-bone cliffs, and narrow pass-forts.
6. **Amber Road:** a chain of market towns and caravan stations linking otherwise distant crowns.
7. **Sunward Coast:** bright harbors, terraced vineyards, bell towers, and the Seven Shrines of the Synod.
8. **Mycelial Weald:** wet greenwood where fungal lanterns mark safe paths and the forest remembers old agreements.
9. **Eastridge Marches:** contested hills and river crossings where Valewyn's roads meet the open frontier.

The exploration interface may present additional realm and march catalogs. Those procedural or game-data entries do not change this high-level political map without a deliberate canon update.

## Calendar and Customs

- **Emberwake:** at the first cold turn of the year, households share a coal from their hearth with a neighbor. The gesture means warmth is a duty, not a possession.
- **First Market:** the Amber Road reopens after winter. Towns post fair weights and safe-conduct notices before the first caravan leaves.
- **Rooting Day:** communities plant windbreaks and repair stream banks. Even crowns that do not follow the Verdant Covenant observe the day as a promise to the next harvest.
- **Seven Bells of Dawn:** the Sunward Coast's bell towers ring for seven public acts of care: clean wells, mend roads, tend the sick, feed travelers, shelter children, bury the forgotten, and reconcile one old quarrel.
- **Night of Quiet Lanterns:** families set a covered light in a window for anyone traveling after dusk. A lantern is a welcome, never a signal to surrender a town.

## Adventuring Orders

Six independent orders help people cross political borders without erasing them. Membership is earned through service; an order may include several peoples and disagree internally about the right course.

1. **Crownroad Wardens:** keep bridges, mile-stones, and village watchfires usable through the border seasons.
2. **Lantern Cartographers:** chart safe passages, mark changed waystones, and publish maps that travelers can actually follow.
3. **Deepdelve Delvers:** survey sealed halls and recover relics under contracts that require returning dangerous finds for study.
4. **Greenveil Keepers:** negotiate access to old woods, tend damaged ley-lines, and protect settlements from hungry wild magic.
5. **Sunbell Hospitallers:** carry healers, clean water, and refuge charters between towns after a siege or hard winter.
6. **Free Company Charter:** gives independent warbands shared rules for contracts, surrender, ransom, and care for civilians.

The game exposes an Academy Wings route for six adventuring orders; the names above are the setting canon for future content and are not all represented as selectable factions yet.

## A Brief History

- **The First Hearths:** settlements form around springs, defensible ridges, and reliable crossings. The earliest wards are boundary marks protecting wells and granaries.
- **The Road-Crown Age:** towns connect through the Amber Road. The great peoples establish courts, holds, and compacts, each with a different view of shared law.
- **The Emberfall:** distant fires appear along several ley-lines. Waystones dim, old routes fail, and forgotten creatures emerge from sealed places. The cause remains disputed.
- **The Truce of Nine Banners:** envoys agree that no crown may claim every broken waystone as its own. The truce slows open war but does not end rivalry.
- **The Age of Embers:** the present day. The roads are reopening unevenly, relics are being found, and local choices determine whether a march becomes a refuge or a battlefield.

## The Five Arts of Magic

The Codex preserves five founder arts: Emberweaving, Verdant Covenant, Stone and Sigil, Dawnsong, and Gloam and Wayfinding. Ten elemental schools add Aether, Nether Ether, Ethereal, Light, Dark, Earth, Fire, Water, Wind, and Null. Each elemental school contains three classes, nine subclasses, and nine spells with ranks, targets, types, subtypes, stats, and effect substats; their generated records are defined in [`src/data/elementalMagicData.ts`](../src/data/elementalMagicData.ts). Players can learn, sell, and practice spells locally. Practice does not resolve campaign combat or provide server-authoritative outcomes.

### Emberweaving

Shapes existing heat from hearth, forge, and flame. It is associated with the Ashen forges and Valewyn's watchfires. Its ethic is restraint: ember can warn, warm, or ward, but fire always has somewhere to spread.

### Verdant Covenant

Works with root, rain, seed, and natural recovery. Sylvan keepers and Mycelium Court tenders share parts of the tradition. It can support living things, but it cannot return the dead or command a forest without consequence.

### Stone and Sigil

Uses marks on real materials to brace, bind, reveal, or read stress. Ironroot rune-binders are renowned practitioners. A sigil needs a surface and a sound design; a broken line breaks the working.

### Dawnsong

Uses light, voice, and voluntary vows to protect, clarify, and comfort. It is practiced in the Sunward Coast and by traveling healers. Its central prohibition is coercion disguised as care.

### Gloam and Wayfinding

Works with shadow, remembered paths, landmarks, and dreams. Its practitioners can help a traveler follow a known route or hide one careful movement. They cannot make an unknown road safe or replace a journey with a thought.

## Magic's Three Laws

1. **A working needs an anchor.** Flame needs fuel, growth needs living ground, a sigil needs a surface, and a path-working needs a known mark.
2. **Every working leaves a trace.** The trace may be warmth, a changed leaf, a fading rune, a remembered song, or a disturbed shadow.
3. **Magic cannot erase responsibility.** It cannot restore the dead, compel genuine loyalty, or make a dangerous choice harmless.

These are narrative constraints for future writing, not implemented spell-system rules.

## Orders, Creatures, and Conflict

The realm's adventuring orders are practical fellowships rather than uniform armies: wardens keep roads, scribes record relics, outriders guide marches, healers tend refuges, delvers map old halls, and free companies accept difficult contracts. Their loyalties can overlap with crowns without being owned by them.

Local threats should reflect damaged places and contested resources: ash drakes haunt warm ravines, rootwights guard violated groves, stonewights echo through abandoned mines, and gloam-moths gather near broken waystones. The Marches Bestiary catalogs nine classes and 90 named field dossiers with habitats, abilities, weaknesses, ranks, and reference rewards. These dossiers are not live encounters; active encounter data remains authoritative for anything currently present in the game.

The central conflict is not a single evil empire. It is a rebuilding world where fear, scarcity, ambition, and incomplete knowledge can turn neighbors against one another. Stories should leave room for diplomacy, repair, exploration, and hard-won trust as well as battle.

## Tone and Naming

- Favor grounded wonder: old roads, small rituals, weather, craft, and local stakes.
- Give each culture internal disagreements and ordinary daily life.
- Use **realm**, **holding**, **march**, **crown**, **warband**, **leyline**, **waystone**, and **relic** in player-facing prose.
- Keep old technical identifiers out of lore text unless explaining a code reference.
- Treat the lore here as canon for new writing; update this file and the Arcane Codex seed together when magic canon changes.
